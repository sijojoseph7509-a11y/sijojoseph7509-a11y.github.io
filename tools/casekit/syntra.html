Syntra in one look

Find a perfume by feeling. Then step inside it, and take it home.

Turn a knob and three sliders to describe a feeling. Syntra names the perfume, lets you step inside it in VR, and prints a ticket that comes alive in AR.

VR · META QUEST 3

Your scent becomes a place.

Nine 360° worlds: a starry lake, a flower garden, the snowy Alps. Put on the headset and stand inside the perfume you just found.

AR · PRINTED TICKET

Take it home.

A thermal ticket with your scent’s drawing. Scan it at home and the drawing comes alive.

Is it worth solving?

Choosing is where the sale is won or lost.

People enjoying a kiosk isn’t enough. I asked whether a store would pay for it, and how I would know.

The cost

Shoppers leave undecided

Testing tires the nose after a few scents, and staff can’t guide everyone. An undecided shopper is a lost sale.

Proof it matters

60% of perfume users find new fragrances by trying them in store, and 88% say the scent itself decides the purchase.

Designing for the highs and lows

A rush, an empty floor and a broken printer.

A kiosk is judged on its busiest and worst moments. Some of these I built for the exhibit; the rest are how I’d handle them in a store.

High · a queue

A crowd at the exhibit

One turn of the knob and the sliders gives a match straight away; VR stays optional, so the line keeps moving.

High · friends together

Three people at one kiosk

Every turn shows on screen at once, so friends compare, and each prints their own ticket.

Low · no staff

One shy visitor, nobody around

The idle screen invites a turn; nothing needs explaining.

Low · not quite right

The match doesn’t feel like them

A small turn gives the next closest scent, without starting again.

Worst · no paper

The ticket can’t print

The screen shows a code to save the result to a phone instead.

Worst · no headset

VR isn’t available

VR is skipped; the match and the ticket still work.

How I made it

With my team, and with AI where it helped.

Syntra was a team of three. My parts: the interaction and screens, the nine VR worlds, and the ticket with its AR.

Arduino + ESP32

The knob, three sliders and the button, programmed in the Arduino IDE on an ESP32.

VR

Unity for Quest 3

Nine 360° worlds built in Unity for the Meta Quest 3.

AI

AI helped me write the ESP32 and app code, and make images and the ticket drawings. The interaction and every choice are mine.

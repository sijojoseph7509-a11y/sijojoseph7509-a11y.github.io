Carekin

Carekin makes sure everyone caring for a parent knows when a medicine changes, and when today's dose was given.

Care + kin · the family that shares the care

WALKTHROUGH · 49 S

From a paper prescription to “Given”, in under a minute.

THE PROBLEM

When a dose changes, only one person hears about it.

Amma takes five medicines in Thrissur. Priya plans them from Bengaluru, Lakshmi gives them at home, Arun worries from Dubai. A new prescription arrives as a WhatsApp photo nobody confirms.

57.8%

of family carers made a medication error in the past year. With several carers at home, the risk triples.

THE SOLUTION

One medicine plan,
seen three ways.

Priya plans in the app. Lakshmi gets each dose on WhatsApp, in Malayalam, with a photo of the tablet and one button. Arun gets a calm weekly note. A changed dose stays open until all three have seen it.

MY ROLE

Research, strategy, interaction and visual design, a design system, the prototype and notes for developers. Self-initiated; not yet tested with carers.

REAL VOICES

I started from what carers and parents actually say. These are verbatim, from published studies.

“She just carries out the tasks, while I make the plans… she observes and reports back to me.”
“She always makes sure to call before I go to bed.”
“I worry that they may not fully understand what I’ve prepared, and sometimes my mom takes the medications without them noticing.”
“Having no one to take you to the hospital is a real crisis.”

WHAT IT DOES

Six features, each answering one moment that goes wrong today.

01 Today at a glance

One calm sentence first, then who gave what, and when.

04 Double-dose guard

If a tablet was already given, Carekin says so before it is given again.

02 Changes nobody misses

The change in words, the actual tablet, old → new, and who has seen it. It stays open until everyone has.

05 Scan, then check

The prescription is read on the phone. Anything it isn’t sure about is shown for Priya to confirm.

03 Doses on WhatsApp

Lakshmi gets each dose with a photo of the tablet, in Malayalam. One tap: Given.

06 Home mode

Optional. One dose, one 96 dp button, dark, readable at 200% text.

BRAND

care + kin. One capsule in two halves with a small gap: mint is the helper who gives the dose, coral is the family far away. The gap is what Carekin closes.

The human friction

When Amma’s dose changes in Thrissur, the worry isn’t in the pillbox. It’s in the hand-offs: A paper slip from the clinic, a WhatsApp group full of good-morning flowers, and a son in Dubai who can’t tell if anyone saw the change.

Medicine apps already exist, but they are built for one patient managing their own pills. Carekin is built for the people around that patient, who share the work but not a single source of truth.

03 — Scenario

A Tuesday in three cities…

One dose changes. Three people need to know. None of them is in the same room.

The problem

In Thrissur, the doctor has halved Amma’s blood-pressure tablet. Lakshmi holds the new slip in one hand and the old routine in her head.

In Bengaluru, Priya scrolls the family group at 9:40 pm. Forty messages, good-morning flowers, and nothing about the tablet.

“Did anyone tell Lakshmi?”

In Dubai, Arun stands at the window. He can’t call at every dose. He only wants to know it was given.

Then, with Carekin…

Priya photographs the new slip. Carekin reads it on her phone; she checks every field herself, then confirms the change.

At 8:00, Lakshmi gets one WhatsApp message with a photo of the half tablet. She gives it to Amma and taps Given. Nothing to install.

“Given.”

Priya sees one calm tick. On Sunday, Arun gets a short summary. Nobody had to ask.

Nobody changed how they live.
The information reached each of them where they already were.

System architecture

One schedule, one event log, two surfaces. WhatsApp is the front door for everyone who isn’t the organiser.

Priya · Carekin app

Android. Schedule, prescriptions, the “What changed” queue.

Lakshmi · WhatsApp

Dose prompts with reply buttons. Photo of each tablet.

Arun · WhatsApp

Weekly digest. Alerts only on unconfirmed changes.

Carekin service

Schedule

Every change becomes a new schedule entry, never an edit.

Event log

Append-only: doses, confirmations, changes. Each entry carries device time, received time and schedule version.

Message router

Turns schedule slots into WhatsApp prompts; turns replies back into events.

WhatsApp Business Platform

Cloud API. Templates, interactive reply buttons, media messages.

dose prompts and replies travel through WhatsApp

Platform constraints that shaped the design

Max 3 reply buttons

So each prompt offers exactly: Given · Not given · Need help.

24-hour service window

Interactive replies are allowed only within 24 h of her last message, so the 8 am prompt is a pre-approved template. Her tap reopens the window for the day.

1,024-character body

One dose per message, with the tablet photo attached instead of a long list.

Prescription intake

Paper stays the source. Carekin reads it, but a person always checks it.

On-device text recognition (Google ML Kit) reads the printed text. The photo never leaves the phone for reading.

Priya checks each field against the photo, pinned beside the form. Never auto-accepted.

5

Goes to the “What changed” queue for every carer to confirm.

Guardrails: handwritten slips often read badly, so the fallback is manual entry beside the photo. Carekin never suggests a dose or interprets a medicine. It records what the doctor wrote.

Sync & conflicts

8:00, Lakshmi logs the morning dose with no signal. 8:05, Priya approves a lower dose. Who wins?

Append-only

Doses are never overwritten. Last writer wins never applies to medicine.

History, not overwrites

The 8:00 dose was right for the old schedule at 8:00.
It stays valid; the new schedule starts at the next slot.

Graveyard of ideas

What I threw away, and why. Each idea failed one of the principles: reach people where they are, never make the helper learn, never hide a change.

Rejected through critique against those principles, not through user testing yet.

Rejected

Push notifications only

The obvious answer. But the person giving pills never installs the app, so the most important reader gets nothing. And a dismissed notification is not a confirmation.

Rejected

A smart pillbox

Solves “was it taken?” for one box. Costs money, needs refilling by someone trained, and can’t tell anyone that the doctor changed a dose yesterday.

Rejected

A daily voice call to the helper

Feels personal, but a call can’t show which tablet, leaves no record, and lands at the wrong moment in a busy kitchen.

Rejected

A shared spreadsheet

Everyone can see it; nobody updates it. Families already proved this with the paper chart on the fridge.

The single most important screen:
A dose changed, and everyone must notice.

Three tries at the “What changed” card

Rejected

Try 1 · A diff table

Precise, but a table to decode at 9 pm. Abbreviations like BD and HS are doctor’s language, not Lakshmi’s.

Rejected

Try 2 · Colour only

Calm and quick, but colour carries all the meaning: red and green fail for colour-blind readers, and nothing says from when.

Chosen

Try 3 · One sentence, one photo, who has seen it

Says the change in words, shows the actual tablet, and makes “not seen yet” visible without blaming anyone.

Before. Accurate, and stressful.

After. A plain-language status, grouped by time, and who gave each dose.

Home mode

The most important user had never been shown the app. Helpers give the tablets, often on a shared phone and in Malayalam, so Home mode shows one dose at a time, a photo of the actual tablet, and one button as big as a thumb.

Prescription changes stay open until everyone taps "I've seen this". A visible "Arun · waiting" is gentle accountability without blame.

Text size

Amma’s helper may set her phone to the biggest text it allows. Android 14 goes up to 200%. Home mode has to stay usable there, not just survive it.

Pinned, not pushed

At 200% the content scrolls but “Given” stays pinned to the bottom, so the one action never falls below the fold.

Large text grows less

Android 14 scales big text on a gentler curve, so the 26 sp title grows less than the 14 sp caption. Hierarchy survives.

sp for text, dp for space

No fixed heights on text containers; every block reflows. Padding stays in dp so the layout doesn’t balloon.

Verify on device

Check every Home mode screen at 200% on a small Android phone before release. Not done yet.

Feedback spec

For someone giving tablets while cooking, the phone has to say “done” without being looked at twice: a fill, a tick and a short, distinct pulse.

Error prevention

The most dangerous mistake isn’t a missed tablet. It’s the same tablet twice, because two people each thought the other forgot.

Name the person and the time

“Lakshmi at 8:02” settles the doubt faster than “already logged”.

Safe choice first

The big button is the one that does nothing. Giving again needs a deliberate second step.

Undo, not delete

Every entry can be undone for 5 seconds, and corrected later with a reason. History is kept.

Not given is a real answer

“Amma refused”, “ran out”, “asleep”. Recorded calmly; Priya gets one summary, not an alarm.

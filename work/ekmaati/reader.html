02 — Statement

Turning a seasonal commodity into a keepsake. A name, a mark and a temple-shaped box for the clay gods of Pen.

My own words on the first brainstorm sheet: faith, artistry, tradition. Every decision after had to carry all three.

03 — The friction

The human

FRICTION

A family brings home a god shaped by hand, from river clay, by makers in Pen. It arrives in bubble wrap and tape, in a box nobody keeps, bought from a row where every idol looks the same.

The craft was never the problem. The missing pieces were a name, an identity and a box that let people see what they were buying, and keep it.

Intricate, river-clay idols from a GI-registered craft, sold like a seasonal commodity: without a name, a maker or a box worth keeping.

Sold

08 — Principles

Used as a filter for every decision below.

Four

PRINCIPLES

From the clay

Every colour, curve and material must trace back to shadu maati or the river.

Faith first

The idol is sacred before it is a product. Nothing on the box sells louder than the god inside.

Keep, don’t discard

If a part of the pack can’t be kept or dissolved, it doesn’t belong.

Small places

Design for a desk, a shelf, a hostel room: compact, stable, beautiful at arm’s length.

Logo

SYSTEM

One lockup, one mark, four backgrounds. Proposed usage rules, drawn from the colour contrast checks.

Never: the mark in sindoor, gold on gold, or the wordmark on photography without a snow panel.

11 — Brand asset

River lines,

A BRAND ASSET

The contours of a riverbed, where shadu maati is dug. Offset loops that never quite repeat: the same idea as the brand, one clay in many forms.

Construction

Offset loops around 2 off-canvas centres, 40–48 px apart, with three slow waves so no two rings match.

Stroke

1.4 px at 1440 px wide; scales with the layout, never thicker than the mark’s thinnest stroke.

Colour

One step from the background: never gold lines on snow, never sindoor.

Restraint

At most once per spread, never behind body text, never touching the idol or the mark.

17 — A day with the box

The Festival Errand…

It was the Saturday before Chaturthi.

The kind of Saturday when every lane smells of marigold and wet earth, and every stall has a hundred Ganeshas looking back at you.

He chose the small one.
It came in a box shaped like a temple.

The monsoon hadn’t finished with the city. He held the box closer than the umbrella.

At home, there was nothing to cut.
A dotted line ran up one side of the arch, over the top and down the other.

“Just tear along the dots?”

He folded the arch down.
The box was already a temple: two pillars, a doorway, and Ganpati inside.

“Oh.”

For ten days, the desk was a shrine.
A diya, a cup of chai,
and Ganpati in a temple that fit between the keyboard and the window.

On the last day, the clay went back to water in a bucket on the balcony.

The temple stayed.

That is the whole idea.
One clay, kept close.

A designed scenario, not a recorded story. Illustrations: AI-generated in my illustration style (Figma AI), art-directed and edited by me.

19 — Accessibility & edge cases

For every hand,

IN EVERY WEATHER

Accessibility

Two languages

Marathi and English on every face, at equal weight.

Contrast with numbers

Text only where it passes: 9.9 : 1, 6.4 : 1, 16.1 : 1.

Not colour alone

The tilak is read by shape and position, not only red.

Easy to open

No tape and no pull tab: tear along a perforated arch on three sides. No knife needed.

Edge cases

Monsoon humidity

350 GSM board; the idol sits on a raised niche floor.

Unbaked clay in transit

The idol sits on a raised base, held clear of the panel.

Visarjan at home

Shadu maati dissolves in a bucket; a guide is printed inside the panel.

Authenticity

A reserved space for the registered GI logo, once authorised.

Proposed specifications and responses; not yet built or tested.
